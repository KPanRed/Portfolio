The first satisfaction readout for Chat — a baseline measurement to understand how branch associates experienced both the live chat tool and the chatbot, and to establish a benchmark for tracking progress over time.

01 — Background

Why This Study

Chat was a new-ish product in the associate toolkit — but without a satisfaction baseline, there was no way to measure whether improvements were working, how the product compared to benchmarks, or where to prioritize future investment.

This study was designed to answer three questions: How satisfied are associates with Chat? Does usage frequency affect that satisfaction? And what single change would have the biggest positive impact on their experience?

02 — Research Objectives

What We Measured

01
Measure Satisfaction Over Time
Establish a repeatable baseline so teams can compare future scores to this measurement and detect real, statistically significant changes.
02
Benchmark Against External Standards
Convert scores to the System Usability Scale (SUS) — the most widely used standardized usability survey — to compare against the industry average of 68.
03
Compare Frequent vs. Infrequent Users
Understand whether heavy users experience Chat differently than occasional users — and whether any differences are statistically meaningful.
04
Surface the Highest-Impact Enhancement
Ask lower-scoring associates directly — what single change would most improve their Chat experience? Capture unfiltered open commentary.

03 — Methodology

How We Designed the Study

Survey Instrument
UMUX-Lite + Custom Efficiency Question
Four core constructs rated on a 1–7 scale: Satisfaction, Need Fulfillment, Ease of Use, and Efficiency. A custom efficiency question supplemented the standard UMUX wording to capture nuance.
Sampling Strategy
Stratified by Usage Frequency
Top 1,500 users (40–6 uses in 90 days) plus a 500-associate sample of low users (1–2 uses). Mix of Financial Advisors and Branch Office Administrators.
Statistical Approach
98% Confidence Intervals
Confidence intervals rather than simple averages were used for all comparisons — ensuring decisions were based on population-level estimates, not sample noise.
"Even if sample average scores exceed previous scores, unless the confidence interval exceeds the goal, it is entirely possible that differences are due to random chance — not a real change in the experience."
— Methodology Note, Survey Report

05 — Survey Scores

How Chat Scored

Associates rated Chat on four dimensions, each on a 1–7 scale. The composite score met the goal of 5.5, falling within the predicted confidence interval of 5.3–5.6. Ease of Use was the standout — the only dimension to clearly exceed the goal. Efficiency was the weakest performer.

06 — Frequency Analysis

Frequent vs. Infrequent Users

Frequent users (10+ chats in 90 days) appeared to score Chat slightly higher than infrequent users — but a T-test found no statistical significance in the difference. The variation is most likely attributable to sampling error, not a genuine difference in experience between the two groups.

Frequent Users (10+ chats)
Slightly Higher
Appears higher across most dimensions — but within the margin of sampling error.
Statistical Finding
No Significant Difference
T-test confirms: differences are likely due to sample selection, not a real gap in experience.
"The difference may be due to the result of sampling error and does not necessarily indicate a difference between the two groups."
— Survey Analysis, Frequency Comparison

07 — Benchmark Comparison

How Chat Compares to Industry

UMUX-Lite scores can be statistically converted to the System Usability Scale (SUS) — the most widely used standardized usability benchmark. The average SUS score across all products is 68. Chat's predicted SUS score met the internal goal of 75 and exceeded the industry average.

What Associates Said

Associates who gave a rating of 3 or below on any dimension were asked an optional open-ended question: "What single enhancement could be made to Chat that would have the largest positive impact on your experience?" 62 comments were analyzed.

09 — Key Takeaways

What This Tells Us

This first readout gives the team both a foundation and a direction. The composite score meeting the goal is a positive signal — but the efficiency gap and chatbot friction point to where the most meaningful improvements can be made.

✓
Baseline Established
Future surveys can now be compared against this readout to detect statistically significant improvements — or regressions.
↑
SUS Exceeds Industry Average
Chat's predicted SUS score of ~75–78 beats the 68 industry average — a strong starting position relative to comparable products.
↓
Efficiency Is the Weak Point
Both the custom and standard efficiency questions fell below goal. This is corroborated directly by open comments — speed and task completion are where associates feel the most friction.
!
Chatbot Trust Gap
62% of open comments centered on bot friction and desire for live agent access. The chatbot is not meeting the bar associates need for complex queries — a clear product improvement opportunity.
Run follow-up surveys at regular intervals to track progress against this baseline
Prioritize chatbot improvements — specifically reducing friction before live agent handoff
Investigate efficiency gap: where are tasks taking longer than expected, and why?
Consider a streamlined "connect to live agent" pathway for associates with complex queries
