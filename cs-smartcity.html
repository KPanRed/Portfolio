01 — Executive Summary

Workshop Outcomes & Strategic Direction

System Soft hosted a design thinking workshop with Hurley Development to validate HQ's vision for the digital experience and create a foundation for the digital experience strategy. The workshop achieved three critical objectives: unified UX strategy alignment, persona and journey map validation, and prioritized use case identification for the first prototype iteration.

Workshop Goals
Validate HQ's Vision: Define unified digital experience aligned with North Star principles. Develop Artifacts: Create personas (tenant, visitor, operator) and journey maps across service areas. Prioritize Prototype: Identify 1-2 high-value use cases per service area.
Key Collaborators
System Soft (UX Strategy), Hurley Development, Dr. Youngchoon Park (Vision), Emmanuel Robert (Product Strategy). Cross-functional approach ensured alignment across vision, feasibility, and user needs.

Core Definitions

What is a Persona?
An archetypical user whose goals and characteristics represent the needs of a larger group of users. Built on primary & secondary research, validated through workshops, personas guide all design decisions and help teams stay user-focused.
What is a Journey Map?
A visualization of the process that a person goes through to accomplish a goal. Maps touchpoints, pain points, emotional highs/lows, opportunities, and moments of delight. Reveals where innovation can have the greatest impact.

02 — HQ Vision Statement

The North Star for All Digital Experience

HQ will be the first "micro" smart city that strives to incorporate a wide range of experiences into one multi-faceted city where people engage in all aspects of life including working, living, and recreating. Innovative technologies will be thoughtfully integrated throughout the city for a seamless, frictionless solution to daily tasks. HQ's guiding principles include climate consciousness, inclusivity, equity, and a dedication to data transparency and honest communication with all in the community.
HQ Vision Statement

Core Guiding Principles — These principles are integral to all aspects of HQ (digital experience, branding, infrastructure, community services). All design decisions must be evaluated against these values:

Value to People
Quality of health, access to shopping/leisure/parks/entertainment. Work-life balance through reduced commute and work-from-anywhere flexibility. Seamless, shared experiences across all touchpoints.
Inclusiveness & Equity
Affordability and accessibility across all demographic and socioeconomic segments. Social inclusion bridging diverse communities. Climate sustainability with quantifiable environmental impact.
Connection & Safety
Connection between citizens, the city, and service providers. Physical security and digital safety. Transparent data management and honest communication about how data is used.
Innovation Philosophy
Innovation is not for innovation's sake. It's about adding value to the lives of citizens. Building a community that supports the collective good over individual convenience.

04 — Journey Mapping

Terry's Complete Day: A Peek Into the Life of a Tenant

This comprehensive journey map reveals Terry's complete experience across 14 distinct touchpoints from encountering traffic during his commute through end-of-day reflection. The visualization captures his current actions, frustration peaks, desires, and critical moments where smart city technology can transform daily stress into seamless convenience. Secondary persona interactions (Tenant/Operator, Tenant/Visitor) reveal cross-functional dependencies and ecosystem opportunities.

Key Insights from Journey Map:

Frustration Peaks (Stages 3-7): Terry's emotional journey peaks when searching for parking, dealing with distance from apartment, and coordinating facilities maintenance. This cluster reveals the critical pain point zone.
Secondary Persona Interactions: Tenant/Operator moment at stage 6 (work order placement) and multiple Tenant/Visitor moments (stages 9-12) reveal that solutions must address cross-functional workflows and dependencies.
Service Area Mapping: Color-coded badges at bottom show which smart services (parking, access, facilities, security, signage, etc.) apply to each touchpoint, revealing overlap and integration needs.
Holistic Solution Required: Success demands integrated approach: assigned parking, rapid facilities response, seamless guest access, safety visibility, and reduced cognitive load about home management.

05 — Prioritized Use Cases for Prototype

Smart Parking, Security & Facilities

Based on journey maps and workshop ideation, 1-2 high-priority use cases per service area were identified and validated with stakeholders:

Smart Parking (Priority 1)
Find Parking Spot When Needed: Tenants access reserved spots; visitors find available designated parking. Mobile app is primary enabler with support for alternative access methods.
Smart Parking (Priority 2)
Navigate Easily to Destination: App-based wayfinding guides to selected spot. Digital signage and spot illumination support seamless navigation and reduce search time.
Smart Security (Priority 1)
Feel Safe at All Times: Tenants have confidence in physical security throughout community. Transparent access controls and responsive support provide peace of mind.
Smart Security (Priority 2)
Easy Emergency Contact: Immediate human response when help is needed. Single point of contact for security concerns and emergencies.
Smart Facilities (Priority 1)
Easy Work Order Creation: Single approach to all apartment maintenance requests. Reduce steps, simplify process, enable quick issue logging.
Smart Facilities (Priority 2)
Efficient Notifications: Residents and operators receive timely updates about issues, resolutions, and maintenance schedules.

Key Workshop Recommendations:

Flexibility is Key: Design for varying levels of user engagement, visitor types (one-time, repeat, long-term), and adaptive experience paths
Transportation Component: Emphasize micro-mobility solutions and shuttle services connecting key community destinations
Target Tenant Demographics: Dual-income, 29-31 years old (no kids yet), plus 45+ downsizers. Include children in visualizations to highlight safety aspects
Ambassador Persona: Consider adding staff persona for community ambassadors providing guided experiences and support

06 — Video & Branding Strategy

Concept Direction & Community Identity

Workshop reviewed HQ's video concept and brand direction with feedback from stakeholders. Recommendations focused on emphasizing city features, community connections, and inclusive design:

Video Recommendations
Decrease emphasis on individual personas; focus on city features and amenities. Show daytime work/life experiences and evening social activities. Highlight connections among citizens and transitions between daily activities.
Branding Principles
Express spiritual element for multipurpose worship/event space without overtly favoring one religion. Use iconography carefully to avoid unintentional offense. Maintain current architectural logo or proceed cautiously with new design.
Design Language
Unexpected value through high-end interface. Connect to outdoors and nature. Draw inspiration from luxury lifestyle publications. Use "Retreat" vs "Resort" terminology under consideration.
Community Voice
Diverse narrative perspectives (balance male/female narration). Inclusive tone reflecting community values. Emphasis on collective good and shared experiences rather than individual benefits.
