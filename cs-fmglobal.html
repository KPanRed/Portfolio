01 — Background

The Challenge

FM Global's Operating Standards (OSs), Operating Requirements (ORs), and Underwriting/Data Requirements (URs/DSs) are the foundational documents that operationalize the organization's engineering and underwriting practices. Historically managed through manual, fragmented processes, the company set out to digitize and productize these documents — a transformation requiring deep understanding of two distinct audiences.

The research had to answer: How do Subject Matter Experts currently author these documents — and where does that process break down? And simultaneously: How do field engineers and other consumers experience these documents day-to-day?

Track 01 — Authorship
Creating the Knowledge
The people who build and maintain OS/OR/DS content. A deeply manual, knowledge-intensive process under constant pressure from industry change velocity.
  • Subject Matter Experts who own and revise standards
  • Leads and managers who oversee revision lifecycles
  • 34 ethnographic interviews, avg. 60 min each
  • Focus: goals, pain points, tools, workflows
Track 02 — Knowledge Management
Consuming the Knowledge
The much larger population who rely on these documents for field visits, risk assessments, training, and underwriting decisions every day.
  • Field Engineers, Account Engineers, Plan Reviewers
  • MOETs, FM Approvals, and specialist consultants
  • 1,789 invitees — 398 responses (22% response rate)
  • Focus: baseline scores, training gaps, future desires

03 — Authorship Findings

SME Workflow

Through 34 ethnographic interviews, four core workflow stages emerged defining how Subject Matter Experts create and maintain documents. Each stage revealed distinct goals, tooling patterns, and significant pain points.

01
Identify & Assess
Monitor industry changes, assess business impact, prioritize what needs updating.
  • Manual, time-consuming scanning of industry news
  • Siloed internal data — no unified view
  • Tacit knowledge lost; no revision history
  • Constant pressure from fast-moving industry change
02
Define & Prepare
Scope the revision, assemble the team, set timeline and priorities.
  • No standard tool to track what needs changing
  • No way to review past decisions or participants
  • Assembling the right team takes too long
  • Scope decisions made informally across many meetings
03
Modify & Confirm
Write, test, and validate revisions across impacted systems.
  • Content management is a constant struggle
  • No effective project management tool for SMEs
  • UW tools not designed for 'what-if' scenario modeling
  • Dozens of downstream calculators impacted by any change
  • Impossible to retire deprecated spreadsheets from the field
04
Execute & Monitor
Publish, communicate changes, track adoption and outcomes.
  • UW cannot respond quickly due to system complexity
  • Current systems don't communicate with each other
  • Very difficult to track downstream impact of changes
  • Changes communicated via quarterly batch updates — too slow

04 — SME Voices

What Authors Said

Across 34 sessions, recurring themes emerged in SMEs' own words — capturing the frustration of operating in a fragmented, manual environment with high institutional stakes.

"I have to email 5 different people just to get access to a document that should already be in my reach. And then wait days for a response."
— Subject Matter Expert, Knowledge Access
"There's no version history. If something changes, you don't know what changed, who changed it, or why. That institutional knowledge just disappears."
— SME Lead, Content Management
"Every time I update an OR, I have to manually hunt down every calculator that references it. There's no map. You only find out something broke when someone calls you."
— Underwriting SME, Modify & Confirm Phase
"Publishing shouldn't be a bottleneck. But right now it goes through only a handful of people who also have to handle XML tagging, formatting, and team coordination."
— SME, Execute & Monitor Phase

05 — Synthesis

Overarching Challenges

Synthesizing across all four workflow stages, four systemic challenges emerged that cut across the entire authorship process and must be addressed holistically in any platform solution.

Challenge 01
Content Management
SMEs manage a sprawling ecosystem of documents manually — OSs, ORs, URs, calculators, checklists, training materials, loss histories, field comments, and update histories. There is no unified content management system, leading to inconsistent formatting, writing styles, and a fragile feedback loop between what gets authored and what field users actually receive.
Challenge 02
Knowledge Access
SMEs cannot access the body of knowledge they need in a timely way. They are forced to visit a physical library that was never fully digitized, email multiple stakeholders for access, and wait days for responses. Valuable institutional knowledge is lost in manual storage processes, and redundant information in documentation creates confusion that is only discovered by accident.
Challenge 03
Publishing
The publication process is handled by only a few people and requires manual upload/download, XML tagging, table formatting, and team operations management. While faster publishing directly improves operations, there is organizational tension between SMEs who want to accelerate updates and those who feel the current pace already overwhelms the field with changes they can barely keep up with.
Challenge 04
Data Modeling
Field Engineers rely on interactive calculators and tables that cannot be embedded in static PDFs — they are currently distributed by email. Underwriting SMEs lack an effective way to model data across a book of business, requiring manual projections and recalculations every time a policy is updated. This fundamental gap between document format and functional need is a core design opportunity.

08 — Knowledge Management Insights

6 Key Improvement Areas

The generative survey surfaced six major improvement areas for how document consumers experience OS, OR, and DS content. Findings were validated with open-ended responses and will inform subsequent qualitative listening sessions.

Training
Insufficient Training on Content
Users receive insufficient training on Data Sheets — especially concerning given that Data Sheets are public-facing documents shared directly with clients. Newer engineers feel underequipped when using these documents in the field.
"Since these are our public documents that we share with clients, we use them a lot — but we almost never discuss them in a training environment."
— Sr. Consultant Engineer
Changes
Pace of Change is Overwhelming
The frequency and breadth of changes makes it very difficult for users to stay current — particularly for standards that are not frequently used. Field engineers describe change fatigue that leads to missed updates and compliance risk.
"The frequency and vast scope of changes makes it very difficult to stay abreast — especially for standards not often used. Slow down the pace!"
— Field Engineer
Functions
Need for Interactive Platform
Users want an interactive, web-based platform where they can work across multiple OS and OR documents simultaneously, integrated with their report-writing tools. The current PDF model feels fundamentally disconnected from how work actually happens.
"The dream interface would be software/web-based where you can interact with different ORs and OSs all at once, with an integrated report writing function."
— Field Engineer
Viewing
Documents Are Too Long & Scattered
Documents are perceived as "too big" with critical bits of information scattered across multiple pages. Newer engineers struggle to review them efficiently before site visits, leading to wasted time or underprepared inspections.
"Way too big with bits and pieces of info spread over many documents. No way for newer engineers to review practically before each visit."
— Account Engineer
Search
Search Needs to Be Intelligent
Users need a dynamic, intelligent search tool that predicts intent and supports both keyword and hierarchical tree-based navigation for different thinking styles. The current search experience feels like it was built for a library catalog, not a knowledge platform.
"Need a searchable platform — a 'Fmoogle of sorts', built on SEO, keyword search sophisticated enough to predict what the user is wanting."
— GMFE
Navigation
Navigation Crashes & Confuses
Current navigation is clunky, crashes often, and requires significant acquired knowledge to use. Hotlinks between OS/OR/DS documents and a simple back button are among the highest-volume requests — basic UX that is currently missing.
"The navigation crashes often and requires significant acquired knowledge. Adding hotlinks and a simple back button would make navigation more efficient."
— Sr. Engineering Specialist

09 — Ecosystem

Who Uses These Documents

OS/OR/DS documents are not standalone artifacts — they exist within a larger ecosystem of materials, tools, and training resources. A unified platform must account for all six user groups, each with distinct use cases and information needs.

Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)
OS owners and colleagues who create, revise, and own the standards. Primary authorship platform users. Responsible for the accuracy and currency of all content.
Field Engineers (FE)
Use documents for pre-visit preparation, during-visit reference, and post-visit analysis. Support and mentor junior engineers. Highest volume users of OS and OR content.
Account Engineers (AE)
Use documents at a higher strategic level; less technical depth required but contextual and cross-reference access is essential for client conversations and risk decisions.
Plan Reviewers
Consult documents for guidance on new construction and renovation project reviews. Need deep cross-referencing between ORs, OSs, and specific hazard-type documents.
MOETs
Managers of Office Engineering & Training use documents as training curriculum. Need consistent, accurate, accessible content to build reliable onboarding programs for new FEs.
FM Approvals
Partner with SMEs on specific technical topics (flood, fire protection, specialty hazards). Require deep cross-linking between OS and approval-specific requirements.
Key Implication
All six user groups must be considered in designing an effective platform. The document ecosystem extends far beyond the authorship workflow — it supports field visits, client risk assessments, training programs, and underwriting decisions. A successful product must serve both the people who create these documents and the much larger population who rely on them every day.

10 — Strategic Direction

Strategic Opportunities

Across both research tracks, the findings converge on a clear set of platform opportunities. These are not isolated feature requests but systemic design opportunities that, if addressed together, would transform how FM Global creates, manages, and consumes its core operating documents.

01
Unified Knowledge Repository
A fully digitized, searchable, and centrally accessible library replacing the fragmented ecosystem of PDFs, emails, and physical archives. Must support intelligent search, cross-linking between documents, and complete version history with decision logging.
02
Authorship Workflow Platform
A purpose-built tool supporting the full SME lifecycle — from identifying a need for revision through scoping, drafting, testing, and publishing — with built-in project management, decision logging, and team coordination so tacit knowledge is captured and preserved.
03
Real-Time Change Communication
Replace the quarterly batch update model with a targeted, real-time notification system that tells the right people about the right changes with context — reducing the noise while increasing urgency where needed. Personalized by role, document ownership, and frequency of use.
04
Interactive Data Modeling
Embed interactive calculators, data tables, and "what-if" scenario tools directly into the digital document experience — eliminating email distribution of spreadsheets and enabling dynamic underwriting projections that update in real time as standards change.
05
Contextual Integration with Field Tools
Integrate OS/OR/DS directly into report-writing and field engineering platforms (Polaris and MyVisit) so that engineers surface the right guidance at the moment of need — during a site visit, not after returning to the office.
06
Analytics & Adoption Measurement
Build in the ability to track how published changes are adopted in the field, monitor exception requests, and measure the downstream business impact of standards revisions — closing the feedback loop for SMEs and creating accountability for change velocity.

11 — Roadmap

Research Next Steps

Six research phases have been scoped to deepen and broaden the initial findings — moving from generative research toward design validation and market context.

01
Qualitative Listening Sessions
1:1 interviews with MOETs, GMAEs, GMFEs, OCEs, Plan Reviewers, and FM Approvals to deepen understanding of their OR/OS/DS experience beyond what the survey could capture — including workarounds, mental models, and ideal state.
02
External User Research
Survey and interview external users — clients, brokers, contractors, and industry/regulatory professionals — to understand their Data Sheet experience and how it affects their perception of FM Global as a risk engineering partner.
03
Field Engineer Contextual Inquiry
Shadow Field Engineers using OR/OS/DS in context alongside Polaris and MyVisit to document the real integration friction and design opportunity — understanding what they reference, when, and what prevents seamless access.
04
OR Integration Study
Document how users currently use Operating Requirements in relation to the report writing platform to design a seamless, integrated experience that reduces context switching and improves the quality of field reports.
05
Data Sheets Experience Design
Research and ideate on the ideal future-state experience for clients interacting with Data Sheets, including personalization, app-format delivery, and how the product could be differentiated from FM Global's competitors.
06
Competitive Benchmarking
Conduct competitive and analogous benchmarking to identify best-in-class patterns for knowledge management, authorship workflows, and document search — drawing from insurance, legal, engineering, and SaaS documentation platforms.
