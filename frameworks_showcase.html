02 — Enterprise Architecture

Governance, BI Tools & System Integration

Enterprise architecture frameworks map the technical infrastructure, governance models, and cross-platform dependencies that enable large-scale organizations to function. These aren't abstract diagrams—they're operational blueprints that guide technology decisions, budget allocation, and strategic roadmaps for platforms serving thousands of users and managing billions in capital.

Governance Layer
Transparency frameworks showing how APIs, BI tools (Anaplan, Looker, Qlik, Tableau) and support systems (Clarity, Rally, IBPI, eBACE, JIRA) align under unified governance structures to serve end users.
Data Flow Architecture
Detailed mappings of sources (ERPs, VAST, CDL, VzTime, LDAP) flowing through transformation layers (Anaplan, MySQL) to reporting outputs (Looker dashboards, Budget vs Actual, Adhoc).
Business Process Alignment
Multi-layer frameworks connecting business processes through supported functions (Demand, Resource, Spend, Delivery, Engagement Management) to support systems and BI tools.
Stakeholder Communication
Visual clarity enabling cross-functional alignment between technical teams, business stakeholders, and executive leadership on complex platform ecosystems.
Governance + Transparency Framework
Multi-tier architecture showing governance layer, BI tools, supported functions (Demand/Resource/Spend/Delivery/Engagement Management), support systems (Clarity, Rally, IBPI, eBACE, Digital Cockpit, JIRA), and business processes foundation. Enables stakeholder understanding of how enterprise systems align to serve end users.
Eagle Current Architecture
Data flow from sources (ERPs, VAST, CDL, etc.) through detailed data layer, GTS POCs workflow, to reporting outputs. Shows 50k-foot view of enterprise data orchestration.
OutSystems Architecture
Enterprise architecture design showing OutSystems role, architecture design framework, service distribution across 3 levels, and Forrester Wave competitive positioning.
Multi-stakeholder Clarity: These frameworks serve technical architects, business analysts, executives, and external vendors—requiring precision in technical detail while maintaining accessible visual hierarchy.
Living Documentation: As platforms evolve, these diagrams update to reflect new integrations, deprecated systems, and strategic pivots—serving as source of truth for organizational technology stack.
Decision-Making Tool: Executive teams use these frameworks to evaluate new technology investments, assess integration complexity, and understand total cost of ownership for platform ecosystems.

03 — Systems Integration Architecture

Data Workflows & Cross-Platform Orchestration

Integration architecture diagrams reveal the technical plumbing that enables enterprise platforms to communicate, transform data, and support complex business processes. These frameworks show how disparate systems—ERPs, project management tools, HR systems, time tracking platforms—orchestrate together to create seamless workflows for capital planning, resource allocation, and project delivery.

PSIM & Integrated Systems
CDL (Common Data Layer) orchestration across Project Eagle (Anaplan, YODA, Looker), GRE (Tririga), and Network Systems (Fuze, Unefi). Shows data flows through Investment Management, Project Systems, settlement, and labor postings—with integration interfaces numbered (I10287, I10008, I10085) connecting to SAP S/4, CATS, HR/MimMaster, and external systems (Pinnacle, Fieldglass, 1HR, VzTime, Clarity).
YO/DA Systems Integration
Centralized user interface (YO/DA) serving as orchestration layer connecting Anaplan (Capital Expense and Labor Material Planning), ERPs (Capital Expense Actuals), VSource (Sourcing Request Form), OneClarity (Project Management Tool), and JIRA (Agile Development Tool). Bidirectional data flows enable project approval and execution workflows.
Integration Complexity
Mapping 10+ enterprise platforms with numbered interface specifications (I10287, I10008, I10085, I0036, I0031) showing exact data exchange points and transformation rules.
Workflow Orchestration
YODA serving as centralized orchestration layer enabling project approval workflows, budget allocation, resource planning, and execution tracking across disconnected enterprise systems.
Data Transformation
Showing how transactional data flows from source systems through transformation layers (CDL, MySQL) into analytics and reporting platforms (Looker, dashboards).
Technical Precision
Interface specifications, data models, and system dependencies documented with enough detail for engineering teams to implement and maintain integrations.
Integration architecture isn't just about connecting systems—it's about creating a coherent data ecosystem where information flows reliably, transformations are predictable, and business processes can operate seamlessly across platform boundaries.

04 — Collaborative Whiteboarding & Ideation

Workshop Artifacts & Service Design

Raw whiteboard sessions capture the messy, essential work of collaborative thinking—stakeholder alignment, service blueprints, ecosystem mapping, and iterative design exploration. These artifacts represent discovery workshops, architecture planning sessions, and strategic alignment meetings where diverse perspectives converge into shared understanding. Unlike polished documentation, whiteboard work reveals the thinking process itself.

Complete Ecosystem Mapping Session
Comprehensive whiteboard capturing multiple strategic frameworks: Architecture design patterns, OutSystems platform positioning, service distribution across 3 levels, Forrester Wave competitive analysis, YODA integration hub diagram, and low-code platform evaluation. Represents full-day workshop synthesizing technical architecture, competitive landscape, and strategic positioning.
Service Distribution Framework (3 Levels)
Detailed zoom into service architecture showing user interface screens, API workflows, and backend system components across three architectural tiers. Collaborative whiteboard session mapping how services decompose from presentation layer through business logic to data persistence.
Four-Phase Design Methodology
Strategic process framework showing end-to-end product development: Ideate (Concept Development, Strategic Planning, Brand Positioning, Storyboarding, Illustration), Invent (Experience Design, Game Design, Environment Design, Product Design, Rapid Prototyping), Create (Software/Hardware Development, Media Production, Fabrication, Production), Activate (Installation, Global Deployment, Touring Logistics, Support/Monitoring, Data Analysis). Icons and deliverables mapped to each phase.
Workshop Facilitation
Capturing real-time collaborative thinking as cross-functional teams (architects, product managers, engineers, executives) work through complex problems together in discovery sessions.
Iterative Refinement
Whiteboard artifacts show multiple iterations and refinements—crossed-out sections, revised connections, and evolving understanding as teams explore solution spaces.
Strategic Synthesis
Combining multiple lenses in single session: technical architecture + competitive analysis + business strategy—enabling holistic decision-making rather than siloed thinking.
Process Documentation
Four-phase methodology frameworks guide teams through complex product development cycles with clear deliverables, phase gates, and cross-functional accountability.
Inclusive Collaboration: Whiteboarding creates space for all voices—junior engineers can sketch alongside VPs, creating psychological safety for honest technical discussion and collaborative problem-solving.
Rapid Iteration: Unlike formal documentation tools, whiteboards enable instant revision, alternative exploration, and visual thinking at the speed of conversation—critical for workshop facilitation.
Shared Artifacts: Photos of whiteboard sessions become reference documentation for distributed teams, async stakeholders, and future team members joining projects mid-stream.
Strategic Alignment: Process frameworks establish common language and shared understanding of how teams move from concept to delivery—reducing friction and enabling autonomous execution.

05 — Impact & Applications

How Frameworks Drive Organizational Value

Strategic frameworks aren't deliverables for their own sake—they're catalysts for alignment, decision-making, and execution. When done well, they compress weeks of confusion into hours of clarity, enable executives to make informed technology bets, and give engineering teams the context they need to build the right things.

Executive Decision-Making
Board presentations and technology roadmap reviews rely on enterprise architecture frameworks to communicate platform strategy, integration complexity, and total cost of ownership for multi-million dollar technology investments.
Engineering Clarity
Integration diagrams with numbered interface specifications give development teams precise requirements for API design, data transformation rules, and system dependencies—reducing ambiguity and rework.
Stakeholder Alignment
Workshop artifacts create shared mental models across business, product, and engineering—everyone references the same frameworks, speaks the same language, and understands how their work connects to broader strategy.
Onboarding Acceleration
New team members inherit organizational context instantly—enterprise architecture diagrams compress months of tribal knowledge into hours of structured understanding.
Risk Mitigation
Integration frameworks expose single points of failure, data bottlenecks, and platform dependencies before they become production incidents—enabling proactive architecture decisions.
Vendor Management
Technical architecture diagrams give external partners and vendors clear integration specifications, reducing implementation timelines and minimizing scope misalignment.
10x
Faster alignment when cross-functional teams work from shared visual frameworks vs. verbal descriptions or written documentation alone. Visual systems thinking compresses communication overhead and reduces misinterpretation.
The frameworks that matter most aren't the most beautiful—they're the most useful. They're the ones teams actually reference in daily standups, the ones executives pull up in quarterly reviews, the ones engineers bookmark for integration work. Great frameworks become organizational infrastructure.
