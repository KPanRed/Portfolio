⚡ The Initial Response

The organization's first instinct was to increase training efforts. The assumption: users struggle because they don't know the system. More training would solve the problem.

02 — Strategic Inquiry

The Research Opportunity

Before investing in training (a costly, time-consuming intervention), I wanted to understand something fundamental: Is the problem truly a knowledge gap, or are there underlying workflow and usability issues that no amount of training can fix?

01

Understanding real usage patterns, not theoretical or documented processes
02
Where does the current workflow create friction, confusion, or workarounds?
Identifying actual pain points versus assumed problems
03
How do associates discover and use available features and resources?
Mapping the gap between available capability and actual behavior
04
What changes would most meaningfully improve associate performance?
Identifying highest-impact design and workflow interventions

04 — Research Strategy

Methodology

Two complementary research methods formed the foundation of this study, each answering different types of questions.

Method 01
Usability Testing
Observe users completing tasks in the actual system. Watch where they click, where they pause, where they get stuck.
Why This Matters
Satisfaction scores tell you what is happening. Usability testing shows you where users struggle and reveals the specific design barriers preventing task completion.

05 — Participant Design

Participant Segmentation by Experience

I segmented participants by tenure in role to determine whether challenges were concentrated among newer users or persisted regardless of experience level.

Segment 01
0–3 Yrs
Newer users still learning the system. Likely to struggle with navigation and feature discovery.
Segment 02
3–6 Yrs
Intermediate users with some proficiency. May have developed workarounds to navigate friction.
Segment 03
6–9 Yrs
Experienced users with deep system knowledge. May have accepted inefficiencies as "just how it works."
Segment 04
9–12 Yrs
Expert users who represent the organization's institutional knowledge of the system.
Why Tenure Matters
The Segmentation Hypothesis
If friction is design-related, it should appear across all experience levels. Users with 10 years shouldn't have dramatically better success rates if the system itself is poorly designed. But if training is the issue, newer users should improve rapidly. By segmenting, we can distinguish between knowledge gaps and design failures.

Theme 01

Time Crunch &
Workflow Misalignment

Service Associates operated in a high-pressure environment where speed and responsiveness were critical. When system workflows introduced friction, associates consistently prioritized helping customers over following the intended process.
Finding 1

Associates Consistently Prioritized Customer Needs Over System Requirements

Salesforce · New Case: Branch/Home Office Associate
!
Friction Point
Form must be completed before help can begin, blocking immediate customer response
→
Observed Behavior
Associates skip account entry, help the branch, and return later to document
Actual Salesforce Interface
Multi-step intake form required before case creation. Associates described feeling forced to choose between following the system and helping customers immediately.
E
Evidence
Associates described situations where branch employees called expecting immediate assistance, often requesting screen sharing or troubleshooting support within seconds of connecting. When caller information failed to auto-populate, a multi-step intake was required first.
O
Observation
Many associates intentionally skipped account entry, launched the case, assisted the customer, and returned later to complete documentation. This was universal across all tenure levels.
I
Insight
This wasn't a training failure. Associates fully understood the workflow. The system optimized for documentation quality; associates optimized for service speed. Whenever those goals conflicted, customer support won, every time.
"I just skip through that account number and add it later because sometimes branches need me to immediately Echo."
— Service Associate, 6+ years tenure
Actionable Recommendation
Redesign the intake workflow to support progressive documentation. Allow associates to initiate customer support immediately and complete administrative requirements as part of the natural flow of the interaction rather than as a prerequisite.
Finding 2

Associates Created Parallel Systems Because They Didn't Trust Salesforce to Preserve Their Work

Salesforce · Case Detail View
!
Trust Issue
No visible save confirmation. Associates unsure if notes persist between sessions.
Case Detail Interface
Associates reported uncertainty about whether data would be saved. This drove workaround behavior: maintaining parallel notes in external tools as backup.
E
Evidence
Associates described moments where they weren't sure if their case notes had been saved. The interface provided no persistent confirmation, creating anxiety about data loss during time-sensitive interactions.
O
Observation
Multiple participants maintained parallel note-taking systems outside Salesforce (sticky notes, Notepad, Word documents) as a backup against perceived data loss.
I
Insight
The system's failure to communicate data persistence eroded trust. Associates weren't avoiding Salesforce. They were protecting against a system they'd learned not to trust. The workaround was rational, not lazy.
Actionable Recommendation
Implement clear auto-save feedback and explicit confirmation states. Reduce cognitive burden by eliminating the need to manually manage data preservation during active customer interactions.
Finding 3

Context Switching Between Multiple Cases and Channels Caused Critical Errors

Salesforce Service Console · Multi-Case View
!
Cognitive Load
6+ tabs open simultaneously. No visual cues to distinguish active case from background cases.
→
Competing Panels
Knowledge, Chat, Contact Details, Cases all visible at once. No clear visual hierarchy
Actual Workflow Environment
Associates regularly managed multiple concurrent cases, chat sessions, and knowledge lookups in a single interface, contributing to attention failures and cross-case errors.
E
Evidence
Sessions revealed associates managing multiple open cases simultaneously, with no clear visual system to distinguish active work from background context. Critical information from one case appeared adjacent to another.
O
Observation
Associates developed personal tab-management strategies (specific tab orderings, color expectations) that didn't match the system's actual visual design, leading to frequent navigation errors and wrong-case documentation.
I
Insight
The interface treated all cases as equally salient. Under time pressure, working memory is limited. The system needed to do the disambiguation work, not the user. Every error was a design failure, not a human failure.
Actionable Recommendation
Introduce stronger visual differentiation between active and background cases. Implement contextual anchoring: persistent indicators that keep associates oriented within the correct case context across interactions.
Theme 02

Cognitive Overload &
Visual Blindness

Under time pressure and managing multiple simultaneous workflows, associates exhibited selective attention patterns, systematically missing interface elements that the system designers assumed would be noticed.
Finding 4

Interface Alerts Competed With Primary Tasks — Pushing Critical Controls Out of View

Salesforce · Current Alerts Panel
!
Visual Competition
Red alerts unrelated to current chat push the comment input far down the screen
↓
Displaced Control
Message input box forced far below fold. Associates must scroll during active conversations
Current Alerts · Conversation Panel
System-wide alerts unrelated to the current chat interaction dominated visual space, displacing the primary work area and slowing response times during live customer conversations.
E
Evidence
The Current Alerts panel displayed system-wide announcements at the top of the chat interface. These announcements were completely unrelated to the active customer conversation below them.
O
Observation
Associates had to scroll to reach the chat input field during live conversations. Multiple participants described losing their place in the chat while searching for the reply box, leading to delayed responses.
I
Insight
Information hierarchy was inverted. Low-priority announcements displaced high-priority task controls. The interface treated all information as equally important, forcing associates to perform the prioritization work the UI should have done automatically.
Actionable Recommendation
Decouple system alerts from task-critical UI. Implement a dismissible alert tray that doesn't compete with the primary work area. Prioritize the chat input field as a persistent anchored element during live conversations.
Finding 5

Search Was Unreliable — Associates Had Developed Workarounds That Added Friction

Salesforce · Search Results Panel
!
Search Mismatch
Chatter returns 11 results. Cases returns 5+. Associates searching for case data get social feed results first.
→
Unexpected Result
Today's Tasks shows empty state — dashboard data not surfaced through search
Search Results · Global Search
Search results prioritized Chatter (social) content over Cases and client data, creating friction when associates needed to locate specific records or case history quickly.
E
Evidence
Search results surfaced Chatter (internal social feed) content before Cases and client data. Associates looking for case history, contact records, or knowledge articles had to filter through irrelevant social results.
O
Observation
Associates stopped using global search for complex lookups. Instead, they relied on Recent Records, direct navigation, or colleague recommendations: all slower, more error-prone paths to the same information.
I
Insight
Associates had lost confidence in search. When a core navigation tool becomes unreliable, users develop costly workarounds and stop expecting the system to help them. Search had become a last resort rather than a first instinct.
Actionable Recommendation
Re-prioritize search result weighting to surface Cases, Contacts, and Knowledge articles above Chatter content. Implement role-based search defaults that match the actual workflow of service associates.
Finding 6

Users Relied on Memory Rather Than the Interface — Existing Features Were Invisible

Salesforce · Case Hierarchy
0%
0/12 Success Rate
No participant successfully created a child case across all 12 sessions
?
Hidden Capability
"Cases for Parent Client Case" exists but associates could not locate it without guidance
Case Hierarchy · Parent/Child Case Management
Related Cases and Parent/Child Case functionality existed but was effectively invisible. 0 of 12 participants discovered or used this feature without prompting during usability sessions.
Salesforce · Knowledge Panel · Einstein Recommendations
AI
Underused Feature
Einstein Recommendations surfaced relevant articles automatically, but associates rarely noticed this panel
Knowledge Panel · Einstein Recommendations
The Knowledge panel with AI recommendations existed and surfaced relevant articles. Associates rarely used it. They relied on memory and colleague advice instead, unaware the system was already doing the work.
E
Evidence
Many participants were unaware of Quick Text functionality. Others struggled to locate chat transcripts and expected them through search rather than navigation. Parent/Child case creation (a documented feature) was attempted by 0 of 12 participants without prompting.
O
Observation
Participants consistently relied on tribal knowledge, prior experience, or colleague recommendations rather than interface cues, even when the interface contained the exact feature they were looking for.
I
Insight
The issue wasn't missing functionality. It was broken recognition. The features existed. The interface failed to surface them at the moment of need. Users had learned to rely on memory because the system didn't provide enough cues to help them locate information confidently.
Actionable Recommendation
Improve discoverability of high-value features through better navigation, stronger visual cues, contextual entry points, and consistent placement of commonly used tools. Specifically surface Parent/Child case creation in the Related Cases panel with a prominent, labeled CTA.
Finding 7

Inconsistent Placement of Shared Features Increases Cognitive Load

Cross-Experience Comparison · Case · Chat · Service Request
!
Case Workflow
Contact details, Knowledge, Comments anchored to left & center panels
!
Chat Workflow
Same information in different positions. Associates must reorient on every switch
Cross-Experience Comparison
The same shared components appear in different locations depending on the workflow. Associates must continuously rebuild their spatial memory rather than relying on consistent placement.
E
Evidence
When moving between Case, Chat, and Service Request workflows, participants frequently paused to reorient themselves and visually search for information they already knew existed. Associates relied on memory and repeated scanning to locate common features because those features appeared in different locations across experiences.
O
Observation
Although associates work across multiple Salesforce experiences throughout the day, shared elements — contact details, account information, comments, case details, knowledge resources — were positioned differently depending on the workflow in use. Each experience appeared to have been designed independently, with little consistency across the ecosystem.
I
Insight
Users do not perceive these as separate products. They perceive them as a single ecosystem used throughout their workday. When common information and actions appear in different locations, associates must continuously rebuild their mental model and relearn where information lives. This increases cognitive load, reduces efficiency, weakens learnability, and makes navigation dependent on memory rather than recognition.
Shared Components Found Inconsistently Placed
Contact Details
Account Information
Comments & Notes
Case Details
Knowledge Resources
Navigation Patterns
Root Cause
Multiple teams designing experiences independently without a shared interaction framework. No cross-experience placement strategy for recurring components.
Opportunity
Establish a cross-experience information architecture and placement strategy for shared components across Service Requests, Calls, and Chat workflows. Consistent placement would reduce cognitive load, improve learnability, strengthen feature discoverability, support spatial memory, and create a more cohesive experience across the Salesforce ecosystem.

Was there any stakeholder pushback?

▾

Two primary concerns emerged from stakeholders:

Concern 1: Research Approach
Stakeholder Assumption

Low satisfaction = associates need better training

→
Their Proposal

Invest in expanded training programs and documentation

→
Pushback

UX said let's test before we invest in training

Our Finding

Training wasn't the issue. Associates knew the system.

→
Outcome

Saved training budget. Focused on workflow redesign instead.

Resolution: By positioning research as diagnostic (not evaluative) and leading with evidence, stakeholders became partners in the discovery process. They participated in sessions and saw firsthand that associates understood the system. They were just navigating competing priorities (customer responsiveness vs. workflow compliance). This shifted investment toward solving the actual problem rather than symptoms.
