01 — Research Overview

Study Objectives

This comprehensive research initiative aimed to measure and understand the quality of user experience across multiple service channels at a major financial services organization. The study enabled leaders to compare product performance to established goals and benchmarks, while identifying specific areas for UI improvement versus training needs.

Primary Question
How effective are current support channels in solving customer problems? Which channels are being relied upon most, and why?
Secondary Goal
Separate UI/design problems from training and awareness gaps to inform prioritization of design initiatives.

02 — Research Methodology

Quantitative Survey Design

We deployed a comprehensive survey instrument to 930 service associates (Bank Officers and Financial Advisors), with a strong response rate of 47.7% (443 respondents). The survey employed confidence intervals (98%) to provide statistically valid insights rather than raw sample averages.

06 — System-Wide Pattern

The Web ID Referral Problem

One critical finding emerged across all six channels: support associates frequently refer users to Web IDs and self-service resources without adding context or tailored guidance.

The Problem
Users typically consult self-service resources (Jones Link, Knowledge Base) before contacting support. When associates then refer them back to the same Web IDs without new context, it signals either lazy support or system failure. It's frustrating and unproductive.
The Opportunity
Train support staff to provide tailored guidance alongside resource links. If a user has already found a Web ID unhelpful, explaining it further wastes time. Instead, offer contextualized answers or alternative approaches.

This pattern indicates that support quality isn't just about channel availability—it's about the cognitive load users face and whether support associates provide genuine added value over self-service.

07 — Critical Insight

Design vs. Training

A key research output was distinguishing problems requiring UI improvements from those requiring better training and onboarding.

Training Gaps (Not Design Issues)
Knowledge Search awareness, Comment Box interactions, Dashboard Reports discovery, Case List Filters usage, Parent–Child Case confusion, Case Transfer procedures — all reflect onboarding failures, not system flaws.
Design Problems Requiring UI Fixes
Response time visibility, department routing clarity, question reading emphasis, complex scenario support in chat, chatbot escalation paths — these demand interface and workflow redesign.
"Associates weren't failing to use the system — they had adapted around it. The goal wasn't just to fix the UI. It was to design something they no longer had to work around."
— Research Core Insight

09 — Recommendations

Next Steps for Product Teams

Based on the research findings, we identified specific, actionable recommendations for improving the support ecosystem:

Response Time Visibility: Add ETA indicators and progress tracking for service requests. Reduce uncertainty and set clear expectations for turnaround times.
Department Routing Clarity: Redesign call routing logic to minimize transfers. Provide upfront clarity on which department owns which problem type.
Chatbot Escalation Paths: Implement intelligent handoff to live agents when chatbot confidence is low. Don't leave users in loops; escalate to humans for complex questions.
Live Chat Screen Sharing: Enable agents to emulate or share screens during complex troubleshooting. This bridges the gap between text-based chat and phone calls.
Knowledge Base Integration: Stop referring users to generic web IDs without context. Train support staff to provide tailored guidance alongside resource links.
Training Program Overhaul: Develop onboarding modules for underutilized features (filtering, reporting, case relationships). Measure training effectiveness through follow-up research.
